# Interview brief: Make WordPress network home and navigation

## The problem

The current Make home is a long directory. Individual team pages provide no obvious one-click route back to Make.WordPress.org and no simple way to switch teams. Contributors who span several teams have to remember or reconstruct the network themselves.

## Decisions from Corey

- Treat `Make WordPress` and the current team as separate destinations.
- Put both in the existing second navigation row. Do not add a third row.
- Use `Make WordPress Community` or the equivalent team name as the switcher label.
- Keep the switcher simple: team names, with favorites first.
- Let contributors select their own teams. Do not infer membership from profile badges.
- Let selected teams shape the homepage.
- Explore both a single layered homepage and explicit audience views.
- Explore both direct-to-team links and gateway cards exposing blog, handbook, meetings, and pathways.
- For V1, show published agendas and recent discussion posts rather than pulling from Slack or generating AI summaries.
- Produce sixteen WordPress-native concepts and four deliberate outliers.
- Include a matching team-page navigation state in every concept.
- Use Community, Design, Documentation, and Training as representative teams.
- Keep interactions light and build desktop-first.
- Use a neutral fictional contributor account.

## Boundary

Cheyne’s exploration concerns individual team home or welcome pages and making contribution entry points clearer inside each team. This study concerns the network-level Make homepage, awareness across teams, and persistent cross-team navigation. Team-owned homepage content is explicitly left unchanged.

## Known uncertainty

The third audience is not defined. A project-overview or leadership view remains a hypothesis, not an invented persona. The prototypes therefore test information models without claiming to represent Karen or any other specific stakeholder.
